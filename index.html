V-JEPA-BEV: Multi-Task BEV Perception with Frozen Self-Supervised Video Features

Masa Duric, Nathan Phan Tuan Linh, Paul Bourgois, Tancrède Lamort de Gail
EPFL CS-503 Visual Intelligence · Spring 2026
Report Project Code nuScenes

Research Question

For multi-task BEV perception, what matters more: temporal video understanding or strong spatial representation?

We compare frozen V-JEPA 2.1 and DINOv3 backbones inside the same BEVFormer-style pipeline.

Abstract

Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) is the dominant scene representation in autonomous driving, providing a unified top-down framework for detection, segmentation, tracking, and planning from a single shared feature map. Its quality is fundamentally bottlenecked by the image backbone, but it has been unclear what kind of backbone matters most: one that understands video dynamics, or one with strong static spatial grounding.

We propose V-JEPA-BEV: a BEV pipeline driven by a frozen self-supervised backbone, paired with a convolutional adapter (Conv + residual blocks) and BEVFormer's deformable cross-attention. We run a staged ablation through adapter design, input resolution, deformable-sampling density, and temporal BEV fusion, and use it to compare two frozen backbones head-to-head, V-JEPA 2.1 (temporal) and DINOv3 (spatial), across detection, map segmentation, tracking, and ego-trajectory.

On nuScenes, our best V-JEPA configuration reaches 0.431 NDS and our best DINOv3 configuration reaches 0.466 NDS, both at ~230 ms / sample, less than half the 530 ms latency of the BEVFormer reference (R101-DCN, 0.517 NDS). The takeaways: (i) spatial pretraining matters more than video-level temporal pretraining for BEV projection; (ii) BEV-level temporal attention is non-negotiable, removing it collapses DINOv3 from 0.466 to 0.286 NDS, with velocity error mAVE rising from 0.461 to 1.109; and (iii) multi-task learning exposes a real gradient conflict between 3D detection and map segmentation, with task compatibility, not just task count, determining whether auxiliary supervision helps or hurts.

I. Introduction

Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) perception has become a central paradigm for camera-only autonomous driving because it maps multi-camera observations into a metric top-down space directly consumable by downstream planning modules. BEVFormer established a strong baseline using deformable cross-attention to lift ResNet perspective features into a BEV grid, augmented with recurrent temporal self-attention. Its ResNet-101 backbone, however, is relatively shallow and trained only with detection labels — it lacks the broad semantic and geometric representations modern self-supervised foundation models provide.

1.1 Why BEV?

Before discussing backbones, we briefly defend BEV as a substrate on three grounds beyond benchmarks.

Unification across cameras and modalities. BEV projects every surround camera into a single shared metric grid, giving an AV full 360° coverage — arguably better than human — and enabling joint reasoning across views that single-front-camera systems (e.g. DriveJEPA) cannot match. The same grid is a natural target for LiDAR fusion (BEVFusion), so our camera-only setup extends to multi-modal without architectural redesign.

Composable heads and diagnostic safety. Each task head is a query plus a small decoder over the same tensor; we share one BEV map across four heads, and additional heads (occupancy, planning) would extend the same way. Crucially, BEV features are inspectable: after a failure, they can be probed offline to determine whether an obstacle was correctly localised (failure is downstream) or absent (failure is in the substrate) — a forensic surface monolithic end-to-end systems lack, and one we consider non-negotiable for safety-critical AV.

The bottleneck we observe is implementation, not paradigm. With a 256-channel BEV map, training all four heads jointly drops detection NDS from 0.431 to 0.279 (epoch 6; see §4.5). We read this as a capacity bottleneck — wider channels, task-specific decoders, or gradient surgery are open follow-ups, not a refutation of BEV.

1.2 Approach and contributions

Two families of visual foundation models have recently shown strong transfer: DINOv3, trained on large image collections via self-distillation, produces rich spatial features; and V-JEPA 2.1, trained by predicting masked video patch representations, acquires temporal and physics-like dynamics knowledge. The natural question is whether V-JEPA's temporal pretraining can improve BEV representations — since driving is inherently dynamic — or whether spatial quality is the dominant bottleneck.

This paper contributes: (i) a staged ablation of frozen V-JEPA features through adapter design, resolution, deformable sampling, and temporal BEV fusion; (ii) a multi-task analysis revealing gradient interference between detection, segmentation, tracking, and trajectory heads; and (iii) a qualitative analysis of the representational and architectural gaps that prevent frozen ViT backbones from reaching BEVFormer SOTA.

II. Related Works

BEV perception

BEVFormer uses BEV queries that interact with multi-camera features via deformable spatial cross-attention and temporal self-attention, reaching 51.7% NDS on nuScenes with an R101-DCN backbone. BEVFormer v2 adds perspective 3D supervision, letting modern ViT backbones match or exceed ResNet variants. Lift-Splat-Shoot and BEVDepth use explicit depth prediction; BEVFusion combines camera and LiDAR streams.

Visual foundation models

DINO and DINOv2 use self-distillation on large image corpora to produce spatially structured features without labels. V-JEPA extends the JEPA framework to video, training an encoder to predict representations of masked video tubes from context, producing features sensitive to temporal dynamics. Their utility as frozen feature providers for geometric tasks like BEV projection is underexplored — our head-to-head puts spatial (DINOv3) and temporal (V-JEPA 2.1) pretraining in a single controlled comparison with all other components held fixed.

III. Methodology

3.1 System overview

BEVFormer's ResNet-101 backbone produces a four-scale feature pyramid at strides $\{8, 16, 32, 64\}$, all of which feed the BEV projection. We replace this with a single frozen foundation model — V-JEPA 2.1 (ViT-B/16) or DINOv3 (ViT-B/14) — whose weights are never updated. A lightweight adapter converts the frozen patch tokens into spatial feature maps; BEVFormer's existing projection lifts them into a 200×200 BEV grid. Only the adapter and task heads are trained.

Figure 1. The VJEPA-BEV pipeline. A frozen ViT-B backbone (V-JEPA 2.1 or DINOv3) encodes each camera; a Conv+4Res adapter bridges backbone tokens to BEVFormer-style feature maps; the BEV transformer fuses them into a shared 200×200 BEV map via deformable spatial cross-attention and temporal BEV attention; task heads read from the same map.

3.2 Adapter design

V-JEPA and DINOv3 produce frozen ViT patch tokens of dimension 768 on a coarse grid. Unlike CNN/FPN features, these tokens come from global self-attention and therefore carry a weaker local spatial bias — a mismatch with BEVFormer's deformable cross-attention, which expects dense convolutional maps where neighbouring locations encode neighbouring image regions. The adapter must both reduce 768→256 channels and refine the tokens into a locally consistent spatial map.

A purely linear projection is limited because it processes each token independently and cannot model interactions between neighbouring patches. Adding local convolutions directly addresses this by mixing information across nearby tokens on the patch grid, which yields a clear improvement. We also explored a gated temporal-fusion variant that combined cached V-JEPA temporal slices through a learned per-token gate; it initially improved over naive temporal reduction, but the gain was not consistent in later experiments and the gated variant sometimes degraded performance once stronger adapters and higher-resolution features were introduced. This suggests the dominant bottleneck is not how temporal slices are weighted, but whether the frozen patch tokens are sufficiently refined into a spatial feature map compatible with BEVFormer. Our final design uses four residual convolutional blocks with hidden dimension 512, which provides the best trade-off between spatial refinement, stability, and computational cost.

3.3 Feature resolution

ViT-B/16 processes images using non-overlapping 16×16 patches. Our default 448×800 input yields a 28×50 token grid, scaling spatial resolution linearly with input size. This denser representation directly benefits BEV projection by reducing quantisation error during deformable attention sampling at sub-patch coordinates.

We evaluate four resolutions: 224×384 (6% of native), 368×656 (17.6%), 448×800 (25%), and 896×1600 (full). NDS improves from 0.344 at 224×384 to 0.375 at 448×800, but plateaus at full resolution (0.375). Given our project's time and compute constraints, the marginal returns of full resolution do not justify the higher caching and training overhead. Although full resolution remains technically feasible under our hardware limits, the smaller footprint of 448×800 offers a practical compromise suitable for edge deployment.

3.4 Deformable attention sampling

BEVFormer's original deformable cross-attention samples 8 reference points per scale across its four-level ResNet FPN, for a total of 32 sampled locations per BEV query. Since we replace the FPN with a single-scale ViT feature map, we sample only 8 reference points total. We swept $\{8, 12, 16\}$ and found no meaningful improvement beyond 8: at stride 16, nearby reference points frequently fall within the same patch and retrieve identical token features, making additional samples redundant.

3.5 Temporal BEV attention

Detecting objects in 3D requires more than spatial features — the model must also estimate velocity and heading, which cannot be inferred from a single frame. BEVFormer addresses this by maintaining a queue of past BEV grids, each warped to the current ego position via odometry, and attending over them to track how the scene evolves over time.

This component is also the key variable that separates V-JEPA from DINOv3 in our study: since V-JEPA is pretrained on video, it carries some temporal signal in its tokens, while DINOv3 is purely spatial. Ablating temporal BEV attention reveals how much each backbone can recover on its own versus how much it relies on BEV-level fusion — results in §4.4.

3.6 Multi-task heads

We attach three auxiliary heads to the shared BEV grid: a convolutional map segmentation head (6 nuScenes semantic classes), an LSTM tracking head that propagates detection queries across frames, and an MLP ego-trajectory head regressing 6 future waypoints. All heads share the same BEV features with no task-specific decoders, so their gradients compete over the same representation — consequences analysed in §4.5.

IV. Results

4.1 Setup

Training and evaluation uses the nuScenes dataset (700 / 150 train / val scenes, 6 surround cameras). The primary metric is NDS, a composite of mAP, mATE, mASE, mAOE, mAVE, and mAAE. All models train on 4×A100 80 GB, batch size 4, up to 30 epochs. Latency is measured on a single A100 at batch size 1. The reference BEVFormer with R101-DCN (0.517 NDS, ~530 ms / sample) serves as the performance ceiling — its backbone is fine-tuned end-to-end whereas ours is frozen and pre-cached as H5 tensors.

Four observations stand out. First, the V-JEPA staged ablation produces large additive gains: +0.083 NDS from a better adapter, +0.031 from higher input resolution, and +0.056 from BEV temporal attention. Second, the V-JEPA → DINOv3 swap at the top of the stack adds another +0.035 NDS (0.431 → 0.466) — meaningful, but smaller than the per-stage adapter / resolution / temporal gains, suggesting the backbone choice is one factor among several rather than the dominant one. Third, removing BEV temporal attention is catastrophic for DINOv3 (0.466 → 0.286, −18 NDS points), the single largest movement in the entire study; we unpack this in §4.4. Fourth, the multi-task block reveals a clear backbone-by-task interaction: DINOv3 retains 0.459 NDS with all four heads active, while V-JEPA drops to 0.279 NDS on the same configuration. The stronger spatial backbone is more robust to multi-task gradient pressure — a pattern discussed in §4.5.

4.3 Latency advantage

Our V-JEPA and DINOv3 configurations both run at ~230 ms / sample compared to ~530 ms for the BEVFormer R101-DCN reference, a 2.3× speedup. The advantage has two sources. First, the frozen backbone is pre-cached as H5 tensors, so inference skips the entire backbone forward pass. In a deployment with fixed sensor geometry this cache can be populated offline or updated lazily. Second, ViT-B with our lightweight Conv + 4 Res adapter (~12M trainable parameters) involves substantially less per-sample compute than ResNet-101 with deformable convolutions.

The NDS-latency trade-off is therefore favourable: our V-JEPA model achieves 83.5% of the reference NDS at 43% of its latency, and our DINOv3 model achieves 90.1% of the reference NDS at the same 43% latency. For latency-sensitive contexts, onboard AV inference inside a frame window, fleet-scale deployment, hardware-constrained platforms, that efficiency cannot be dismissed because absolute NDS is lower. The reference BEVFormer is also fine-tuned end-to-end, which is inherently more expensive both at training and inference time; we trade some peak performance for dramatically lower compute cost.

4.4 The critical role of BEV temporal attention

The largest single-factor movement in our entire study is what happens when BEV temporal attention is removed from DINOv3: NDS collapses from 0.466 → 0.286, an 18-point swing. Drilling into the sub-metrics shows what fails.

Velocity is the primary failure mode. Without temporal attention DINOv3's mAVE rises to 1.109, nearly 3× its 0.461 value with temporal attention turned on. This makes physical sense: velocity simply cannot be estimated from a single frame without an explicit motion cue. DINOv3 is a purely spatial image encoder with no mechanism to infer object motion. BEVFormer's temporal attention solves this by aligning BEV grids across time, effectively providing optical-flow information at the BEV level.

Orientation also degrades. mAOE rises from 0.445 to 0.606 (+36%). Predicting heading from a single-frame BEV query is ambiguous: a parked car and a driving car may look identical in any single frame, but their heading is disambiguated by motion history. Temporal BEV attention provides exactly that disambiguation.

V-JEPA is partially robust to this failure. V-JEPA's mAVE without BEV temporal attention is 0.663, against DINOv3's 1.109. This confirms that V-JEPA's video-level pretraining does embed some velocity signal in its features, just not enough to compensate for missing BEV temporal fusion entirely. V-JEPA's temporal pretraining is a partial substitute for BEV temporal attention; DINOv3 has no such fallback.

Design principle. For camera-only BEV perception, BEV-level temporal modelling is not optional. Regardless of how rich the backbone features are, single-frame BEV projection is fundamentally underdetermined for dynamic-attribute estimation. The BEV temporal attention module, despite being a small component of the overall architecture, carries disproportionate responsibility for velocity and orientation.

4.5 Multi-task learning analysis

Training all four task heads jointly produces a sharp backbone-by-multi-task interaction. With V-JEPA, detection NDS drops to 0.279 at epoch 6 (training ongoing) — well below the 0.431 detection-only configuration. With DINOv3 + temporal BEV, the same four-task setup retains 0.459 NDS, almost matching its detection-only ceiling of 0.466. Both models already show meaningful multi-task behaviour even pre-convergence: structured drivable-area and walkway masks emerge, the tracking head propagates identities across frames, and ego trajectories are produced (V-JEPA ego ADE 1.32, DINOv3 ego ADE 1.53). The interesting question is which task combinations interfere, and why.

Selectively combining tasks (with V-JEPA + temporal BEV) reveals a clear gradient-interference pattern:

Detection + Tracking + Ego-trajectory: 0.405 NDS. Only −0.026 versus detection-only, while adding full motion-estimation capability. Tracking and ego-trajectory both depend on the same geometric BEV features that detection wants, so their gradients are largely aligned with the detection objective.
Detection + Map segmentation: 0.360 NDS. A much larger cost (−0.071 versus detection-only). Segmentation gradients push BEV features toward semantic boundary sharpness rather than geometric localisation precision. These objectives are not aligned — a feature that perfectly segments the drivable-area boundary need not localise a car's 3D centre accurately.

Implication. Map segmentation and 3D detection demand different inductive biases in the BEV representation, and naively sharing a single 256-channel BEV feature map across both introduces a bias conflict. The fact that DINOv3 absorbs this conflict more gracefully than V-JEPA (0.459 vs 0.279 NDS in the all-4 setting) suggests that strong spatial features are not only better for detection but also more robust to gradient pressure from heterogeneous heads. Future work should explore task-specific BEV decoders, gradient surgery, or uncertainty-weighted loss balancing — but the underlying lesson is that task count alone is not the bottleneck; task compatibility is.

V. Discussion

Our results raise two deeper questions: why does V-JEPA underperform DINOv3 despite its temporal pretraining, and why do both frozen models fall short of the BEVFormer SOTA?

5.1 Why V-JEPA underperforms DINOv3

On the surface, V-JEPA looks better suited for AV: it understands video, motion, and dynamics. Yet DINOv3 wins across all configurations (0.466 vs. 0.431 at best, 0.459 vs. 0.279 in the all-4-tasks setting). Three factors explain this.

BEV projection is primarily a dense geometric problem, not a temporal one. Deformable cross-attention samples sparse reference points in image space; every sampled location must carry accurate local geometry so that the BEV query places the object at the correct 3D position. DINOv3's self-distillation directly optimises spatial discriminability across the image plane and produces features with sharp spatial structure. V-JEPA's predictive objective rewards more global, contextual representations — useful for dynamics, less precise for pixel-level localisation.

V-JEPA features are temporally entangled. V-JEPA processes a clip of $T=2$ frames jointly; its token representations mix spatial and temporal information. When we extract features for BEVFormer, we use only the spatial tokens from the target frame. However, these tokens have been influenced by the neighbouring frames during the self-attention computation inside V-JEPA, meaning their spatial meaning is partially diluted by temporal context. This can hurt the precision of deformable attention sampling: a token that "knows" about the motion of a pedestrian over 2 frames may not pinpoint the pedestrian's exact spatial location within the current frame as sharply as a purely spatial encoder would.

Domain gap. V-JEPA 2.1 is trained predominantly on web video (Kinetics / HowTo100M-style), with substantially different statistics from automotive forward cameras. DINOv3, trained on a curated billion-image corpus, has broader photometric coverage closer to nuScenes' fisheye-corrected automotive imagery.

Implications. These findings suggest that V-JEPA's temporal knowledge is not effectively exploited by BEVFormer's existing BEV temporal attention module, which operates at the BEV feature level rather than consuming V-JEPA's cross-frame token relationships directly. A more tailored integration — using V-JEPA's attention maps as motion priors for the deformable sampling offsets, or routing V-JEPA features specifically to the tracking and velocity heads — could better exploit its temporal properties.

5.2 Why neither model reaches BEVFormer SOTA

Our best configuration (DINOv3, 0.466 NDS) falls 5.1 NDS points short of BEVFormer v1's 0.517 baseline. Rather than a deficit in backbone quality, this gap is a structural byproduct of our design choices: frozen weights, resolution downsampling, and reduced sampling density.

Feature adaptation and the frozen backbone. While BEVFormer v1 fine-tunes its ResNet-101 end-to-end with BEV 3D detection loss, our backbones remain frozen. End-to-end gradients shape backbone features specifically for perspective-to-BEV transformation (e.g. perspective depth cues, object scale boundaries). Our lightweight Conv+4Res adapter (~12M parameters) cannot fully re-purpose general-purpose, frozen 768-dimensional tokens into these highly specialised multi-scale spatial representations.

Resolution and sampling bottlenecks. Our models use a 4× downsampled resolution of 448×800 (yielding a 28×50 token grid) and 8 deformable sampling points, whereas BEVFormer v1 uses 900×1600 and 32 sampling points. The stride-16 patch tokenisation inherently misses high-frequency, stride-8 spatial details needed for precise boundary localisation. While early isolated ablations of full resolution and higher sampling density showed minimal gain, these were conducted without the temporal BEV module. Combined with temporal context and backbone unfreezing, higher resolution and denser sampling are likely necessary to close the localisation gap — though at significant latency cost.

Missing perspective supervision. Finally, as established by BEVFormer v2, optimal adaptation of ViT encoders to BEV projection requires auxiliary perspective 3D supervision during backbone training to teach the encoder projective geometry and depth cues. Lacking this auxiliary perspective signal, alongside frozen weights and compressed resolutions, constitutes a hard ceiling on our models' geometric capabilities.

VI. Conclusion

We presented VJEPA-BEV, a systematic study of frozen foundation-model features for BEV perception on nuScenes. A staged ablation yields 0.431 NDS for V-JEPA and 0.466 NDS for DINOv3, both at 230 ms/sample versus 530 ms for the BEVFormer reference. DINOv3 outperforms V-JEPA because BEV projection demands spatially precise, non-entangled features — something image-level self-distillation produces more directly than V-JEPA's temporally predictive objective. The same spatial advantage carries into multi-task training, where DINOv3 retains 0.459 NDS across all four heads while V-JEPA drops to 0.279 (epoch 6, ongoing). Neither model reaches BEVFormer SOTA, primarily because of frozen-backbone non-adaptation, our 448×800 / stride-16 / 8-point compression, and the absence of perspective 3D supervision. Frozen foundation-model backbones nonetheless offer a compelling 2.3× efficiency advantage at 83–90% of SOTA NDS, making them attractive for latency-constrained deployment.
