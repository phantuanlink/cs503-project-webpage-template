V-JEPA-BEV: Multi-Task BEV Perception with Frozen Self-Supervised Video Features

Masa Duric, Nathan Phan Tuan Linh, Paul Bourgois, Tancrède Lamort de Gail
EPFL CS-503 Visual Intelligence · Spring 2026
Report Video Project Code nuScenes Website Code

V-JEPA-BEV replaces the trained image backbone in BEV perception with a frozen self-supervised video encoder, producing a shared BEV representation that lifts four downstream tasks at once.

Abstract

Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) is the dominant scene representation in autonomous driving, providing a unified top-down framework for detection, segmentation, tracking, and planning from a single shared feature map. Its quality is bottlenecked by conventional image backbones that process frames in isolation, discarding temporal context and producing spatially inconsistent features.

We propose V-JEPA-BEV, replacing the conventional backbone with a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 video encoder whose dense, temporally consistent features are learned from video without labels. A lightweight adapter and cross-attention module lift the resulting per-camera tokens into a shared BEV feature map, which feeds four task heads simultaneously: 3D object detection, HD map segmentation, instance tracking, and trajectory prediction. We additionally introduce an ego-consistency auxiliary loss that adds no inference cost.

I. Introduction

Autonomous driving systems rely on accurate perception of the surrounding environment, and Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) has emerged as the dominant scene representation because it provides a unified top-down spatial framework for multiple downstream tasks. A single shared BEV feature map can drive detection, segmentation, tracking, and motion prediction simultaneously, enabling joint reasoning across multi-camera inputs without redundant per-task encoders.

The quality of this representation is fundamentally bottlenecked by the image backbone. BEVFormer v2 observes that BEV supervision provides only an indirect gradient to the backbone — the task loss travels backward through multiple transformer layers before reaching backbone weights, giving insufficient signal for a general 2D backbone to learn 3D scene properties. The resulting BEV features are spatially noisy, temporally inconsistent, and biased toward whichever downstream task dominates the loss, degrading every task that shares the representation.

Because the BEV feature map is shared across all tasks, a weak backbone hurts everything simultaneously, which makes fixing the backbone a higher-leverage intervention than improving any individual task head. Existing solutions either require expensive depth pre-training or an auxiliary perspective detection head, adding significant complexity. We ask a simple question:

Can a frozen self-supervised video backbone, requiring no task-specific pre-training, provide the spatial grounding and temporal consistency needed for high-quality multi-task BEV perception?

Our answer is V-JEPA-BEV, a pipeline that pairs a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 video encoder with a lightweight trainable BEV transformer and four task heads. We make three contributions: (i) a V-JEPA-driven BEV pipeline with an adapter and temporal gate that absorbs the resolution mismatch between V-JEPA's coarse patch grid and standard BEV features, (ii) an ego-consistency auxiliary loss that exploits known ego pose to regularise the trainable layers without adding inference cost, and (iii) a simultaneous evaluation across detection, segmentation, tracking, and trajectory prediction — the joint improvement is our evidence that the representation is genuinely better rather than re-weighted.

II. Related Works

Multi-camera BEV perception

BEVFormer establishes the standard multi-camera BEV pipeline by lifting per-view image features into a shared BEV feature map through spatial cross-attention with learnable BEV queries, then refining across time with a deformable temporal self-attention layer. Most subsequent BEV systems retain this template and vary the backbone, the projection geometry, or the task heads. We adopt BEVFormer's transformer and deformable-attention skeleton unchanged, and intervene only at the backbone.

The backbone-supervision gap

BEVFormer v2 identifies that BEV supervision provides insufficient gradient to adapt a generic image backbone to 3D scene properties, and addresses this by adding a perspective detection head that supervises the backbone directly in 2D. They report a persistent ≈3% NDS gap that does not close with extended training. Our approach is complementary: rather than adding auxiliary supervision to train the backbone, we use a frozen backbone whose features are already spatially and temporally grounded.

Generative refinement of BEV features

BEVDiffuser takes the orthogonal approach of leaving the backbone alone and refining the BEV features themselves with a training-time diffusion denoiser conditioned on ground-truth object layouts, reporting +12.3% mAP on detection. Their refiner is discarded at inference. We adopt this teacher–student framing for our stretch goal but replace diffusion with flow matching, and extend the evaluation from detection-only to all four tasks.

Self-supervised video representations

V-JEPA 2.1 learns dense, temporally consistent patch features from internet video without labels, using a context-prediction objective in feature space. The 2.1 release reports 47.9 mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation under linear probing (vs. 22.2 for V-JEPA 1.0), strong evidence that the encoder learns spatially meaningful features rather than only sequence-level summaries. To our knowledge, V-JEPA 2.1 has not been applied to multi-camera BEV perception. Our work combines this frozen video backbone with lightweight auxiliary supervision and a multi-task evaluation protocol.

III. Methodology

3.1 Pipeline overview

Figure 1 summarises the full pipeline. Six surround-camera views are encoded independently by a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B encoder, producing a temporally extended per-camera token grid. A lightweight adapter projects these tokens into the BEV transformer's working dimension; an optional token-wise temporal gate fuses V-JEPA temporal slices. The BEV transformer then lifts the multi-view tokens into a shared BEV feature map $X^{\mathrm{BEV}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$ using deformable spatial cross-attention and BEVFormer-style temporal self-attention. Four task heads read from this shared feature map: 3D object detection, HD map segmentation, per-agent motion prediction, and ego-trajectory prediction. Training adds an ego-consistency auxiliary loss with no inference cost.

Figure 1. The V-JEPA-BEV pipeline. Frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encodes each camera; a trainable adapter and temporal gate convert cached features into BEVFormer-style image features; the BEV transformer fuses them into a shared BEV map; four task heads read from the same map. The ego-consistency loss (training only) regularises the trainable layers via known ego pose.

3.2 Frozen V-JEPA 2.1 backbone

We use V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B as our image-feature extractor and freeze it entirely throughout training. Each camera view is passed through the encoder as a short clip; the encoder produces $T$ temporal slices of $14 \times 24$ patch tokens at $d_v = 768$ channels per slice, giving a cached tensor of shape $[B, 6, T \cdot 14 \cdot 24, d_v]$. Freezing the backbone has three motivations:

Scientific. The point of the project is to test whether off-the-shelf video SSL features are sufficient — fine-tuning would conflate "good SSL representation" with "well-adapted fine-tuned representation".
Computational. A frozen backbone lets us precompute and cache V-JEPA features once per scene and reuse them across all training runs and ablations, turning the per-run cost into BEV-transformer training time.
Methodological. Frozen features that already work are stronger evidence of representation quality than features that are coerced into working by gradient flow.

3.3 Adapter and temporal fusion

V-JEPA tokens live at a coarser resolution (14×24 per camera, $d_v = 768$) than BEVFormer expects (256-dim feature maps). We bridge this with a residual convolutional adapter operating on the V-JEPA spatial grid: a 768 → 512 → 256 stem followed by four residual 3×3 conv blocks, each zero-initialised so the adapter starts close to identity. There is no final non-linearity — BEVFormer's attention layers expect signed features.

To exploit V-JEPA's multiple temporal slices we compare three reduction strategies: last takes only the most recent slice, mean averages across slices, and gated applies a token-wise sigmoid gate $G = \sigma(f([F_{\text{curr}}, F_{\text{prev}}, F_{\text{curr}} - F_{\text{prev}}]))$ that predicts a per-token fusion weight between current and previous slices, producing $\text{fused} = (1 - G)\,F_{\text{curr}} + G\,F_{\text{prev}}$. The gated variant lets the model attend more heavily to the previous slice for occluded regions while preferring the current slice for fast-moving regions. We compare all three in Section IV.

3.4 BEV transformer with deformable attention

The BEV transformer follows BEVFormer's design. Learnable BEV queries arranged on a $H \times W$ grid (default 200×200) interact with adapter features through two attention mechanisms per encoder layer.

Spatial cross-attention projects each BEV query's 3D pillar into every camera via known intrinsics and extrinsics, producing a small set of reference points per query–camera pair. Deformable attention then refines around each reference point: a linear layer on the query predicts $H \times L \times P$ sampling offsets and matching attention weights, and bilinear sampling fetches values at those offset locations. This keeps spatial cross-attention $O(N_{\text{query}} \cdot H \cdot P)$ rather than $O(N_{\text{query}} \cdot N_{\text{tokens}})$, and crucially seeds the layer with a geometrically valid starting point rather than asking it to discover 3D-to-2D correspondences from scratch.

Temporal self-attention fuses the current BEV queries with the previous frame's BEV feature map warped into the current ego frame via known pose. Dynamic objects are intentionally not warped — the layer learns to integrate their inconsistent positions into a motion-aware representation. V-JEPA's temporal slices and BEVFormer's BEV-level temporal fusion are complementary: the former gives temporally consistent image features within a clip, the latter propagates BEV-level state across BEVFormer frames.

3.5 Shared BEV, four task heads

All four task heads consume the same BEV feature map $X^{\mathrm{BEV}}$. Heads are independently registered modules, and the detector instantiates them conditionally based on its config, so any subset can be enabled without touching code. Three of the four heads are designed to be lightweight, isolating the headline question — whether the backbone is the bottleneck — from confounders introduced by heavy task-specific decoders.

3D object detection. A DETR3D-style decoder with 900 object queries, six decoder layers, and Hungarian matching. Predicts 3D box parameters, class scores, and velocity.
HD map segmentation. A three-layer convolutional decoder (256 → 128 → 64 → $C$) producing per-pixel sigmoid logits for six nuScenes map classes (drivable_area, ped_crossing, walkway, stop_line, carpark_area, divider). Loss is sigmoid focal ($\gamma=2$, $\alpha=0.25$). No upsampling — the BEV grid is already at the target resolution.
Per-agent motion prediction. A shared MLP on the 900 detection-query features produces six future waypoints per query in each agent's local frame. Supervision is applied only on Hungarian-matched positives and only on moving agents (speed > 0.5 m/s), which prevents the stationary majority from trivially driving the loss to zero.
Ego trajectory. A single learnable ego query, offset-initialised by an 18-D CAN-bus prior (speed, yaw, acceleration), cross-attends over the BEV map through two decoder layers and predicts six future ego positions via a small MLP.

3.6 Ego-consistency auxiliary loss

The trainable layers downstream of the frozen backbone — adapter, BEV transformer, task heads — can still drift to produce temporally inconsistent BEV maps even when the backbone is itself stable. To regularise these layers without any inference cost, we add an ego-consistency loss: for consecutive frames $t-1$ and $t$ with ego transform $T_{t-1 \to t}$, the previous BEV map warped into the current frame should match the current BEV map at static scene cells.

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{ego}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_{\text{static}}|} \left\| \mathcal{M}_{\text{static}} \odot \Big( \mathrm{warp}\big(X^{\mathrm{BEV}}_{t-1},\, T_{t-1 \to t}\big) - X^{\mathrm{BEV}}_t \Big) \right\|^2 \]

$\mathcal{M}_{\text{static}}$ is constructed from GT 3D-box footprints rasterised onto the BEV grid (plus a small margin) and then inverted — dynamic regions are excluded because ego-pose warping does not capture their motion. Cells with no valid source after warping are also excluded. The loss is added with a small weight to the joint multi-task objective and is purely a training-time term.

3.7 Stretch goal: flow-matching refinement teacher

Following the teacher–student pattern of BEVDiffuser, we explore a flow-matching refinement module as a stretch goal. A DiT operating on BEV tokens, conditioned on GT object layouts, is trained in Phase 1 jointly with the task heads to map imperfect BEV features $X^{\mathrm{BEV}}$ toward a cleaner, layout-consistent target $\hat{X}^{\mathrm{BEV}}$. In Phase 2, the refiner is frozen and used as a teacher: a fresh fusion head is trained with a BEV alignment loss $\| \hat{X}^{\mathrm{BEV}} - X^{\mathrm{BEV}} \|^2$ added to the task losses, and the refiner is discarded at inference. We choose flow matching over diffusion for faster sampling and a simpler training objective; we follow BEVDiffuser's input and conditioning recipe and contribute only the multi-task evaluation protocol.

IV. Results

4.1 Experimental setup

We evaluate on the nuScenes dataset (1000 driving scenes, six surround cameras at 1600×900, 20-second clips with 2 Hz keyframe annotations). All training and validation use the standard nuScenes splits. We report mAP and NDS for 3D detection, mIoU for HD map segmentation, AMOTA for tracking, and ADE / FDE for both per-agent motion and ego-trajectory prediction. Training is performed on 4×NVIDIA V100 GPUs (EPFL Izar cluster) with BEV resolution 200×200 and embedding dimension 256. V. Conclusion and Limitations

5.1 Implications

V-JEPA-BEV tests whether a frozen self-supervised video backbone can substitute for task-supervised backbones in safety-critical perception without inference overhead. Our results [TODO: confirm / partially confirm] this. The implication for BEV system design is that backbone adaptation may not require either expensive depth pre-training or auxiliary perspective heads — a sufficiently strong, frozen video representation closes the same gap. The implication for self-supervised learning is that the dense-feature gains reported on 2D semantic segmentation transfer to multi-task 3D scene perception, suggesting further gains as video SSL backbones continue to scale.

5.2 Limitations

Domain gap. V-JEPA 2.1 is pre-trained on general internet video, not driving footage. Some of the gap we observe between Row A and BEVFormer-tiny on [TODO: which metric] may reflect this; a driving-specific video SSL backbone would likely close it further.
Multi-task interactions. Although Row C improves on Rows A–B, individual task losses do compete during training. We mitigate this by adding tasks incrementally and tuning per-head loss weights manually; a principled multi-task balancing scheme is left to future work.
Single dataset. Our evaluation is limited to nuScenes. Cross-dataset transfer to Waymo or Argoverse would test how much of the gain is dataset-specific.
Compute. Training on 4×V100 fits the project budget but constrains the ablation surface. A larger backbone (ViT-L), additional auxiliary losses, or extended hyperparameter sweeps would require more compute than this setup supports.
Negative result handling. If Row A does not improve over BEVFormer-tiny, this is itself a meaningful empirical finding about the transferability of off-the-shelf video SSL to BEV perception, and we report it as such rather than as a failed experiment.

5.3 Future work

Three directions follow naturally. First, scaling the backbone to ViT-L or larger and quantifying the slope of gains with backbone size. Second, pre-training V-JEPA on a mixture of internet video and driving footage to close the residual domain gap. Third, replacing the GT-conditioned flow-matching refiner with a self-supervised alternative — for example a refiner conditioned on detected (rather than ground-truth) layouts — which would remove the only training-time dependence on labels in our auxiliary objectives.
