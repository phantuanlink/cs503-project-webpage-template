\section{Abstract}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

We investigate whether video and image foundation models---V-JEPA 2.1 and DINOv3---can serve as frozen backbones for Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) perception in autonomous driving. Building on the BEVFormer framework, we run a systematic design exploration over adapter architectures, input resolution, deformable sampling, and temporal BEV modeling. Our best V-JEPA configuration reaches \textbf{0.431 NDS} and our best DINOv3 configuration reaches \textbf{0.466 NDS} on the nuScenes validation set, both at $\sim$\textbf{230\,ms per sample}---under half the \textbf{530\,ms} latency of the BEVFormer reference (R101-DCN, 0.517 NDS). Analysis shows that spatial pretraining matters more than video-level temporal pretraining for BEV projection, that BEV-level temporal attention is the single most impactful design choice, and that its removal collapses velocity and orientation metrics. We further study multi-task extensions (map segmentation, instance tracking, ego-trajectory) and show that task compatibility critically determines whether auxiliary supervision helps or hurts detection.

\section{1. Introduction}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) perception has become a central paradigm for camera-only autonomous driving because it maps multi-camera observations into a metric top-down space directly consumable by downstream planning modules. BEVFormer~\cite{bevformer} established a strong baseline using deformable cross-attention to lift ResNet perspective features into a BEV grid, augmented with recurrent temporal self-attention. Its ResNet-101 backbone, however, is relatively shallow and trained only with detection labels---it lacks the broad semantic and geometric representations modern self-supervised foundation models provide.

\subsection{1.1 Why BEV?}

Before discussing backbones, we briefly defend BEV as a substrate on three grounds beyond benchmarks.

\textbf{Unification across cameras and modalities.} BEV projects every surround camera into a single shared metric grid, giving an AV full 360$^\circ$ coverage---arguably better than human---and enabling joint reasoning across views that single-front-camera systems~\cite{drivejepa} cannot match. The same grid is a natural target for LiDAR fusion (BEVFusion~\cite{bevfusion}), so our camera-only setup extends to multi-modal without architectural redesign.

\textbf{Composable heads and diagnostic safety.} Each task head is a query plus a small decoder over the same tensor; we share one BEV map across four heads, and additional heads (occupancy, planning) would extend the same way. Crucially, BEV features are \emph{inspectable}: after a failure, they can be probed offline to determine whether an obstacle was correctly localised (failure is downstream) or absent (failure is in the substrate)---a forensic surface monolithic end-to-end systems lack, and one we consider non-negotiable for safety-critical AV.

\textbf{The bottleneck we observe is implementation, not paradigm.} With a 256-channel BEV map, training all four heads jointly drops detection NDS from 0.431 to 0.290 (epoch 6; see §4.5). We read this as a capacity bottleneck---wider channels, task-specific decoders, or gradient surgery~\cite{grad_surgery} are open follow-ups, not a refutation of BEV.

Two families of visual foundation models have recently shown strong transfer: DINOv3~\cite{dinov2}, trained on large image collections via self-distillation, produces rich spatial features; and V-JEPA~2.1~\cite{vjepa}, trained by predicting masked video patch representations, acquires temporal and physics-like dynamics knowledge. The natural question is whether V-JEPA's temporal pretraining can improve BEV representations---since driving is inherently dynamic---or whether spatial quality is the dominant bottleneck.

This paper contributes: \textbf{(i)} a staged ablation of frozen V-JEPA features through adapter design, resolution, deformable sampling, and temporal BEV fusion; \textbf{(ii)} a multi-task analysis revealing gradient interference between detection, segmentation, tracking, and trajectory heads; and \textbf{(iii)} a qualitative analysis of the representational and architectural gaps that prevent frozen ViT backbones from reaching BEVFormer SOTA.

\section{2. Related Work}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{BEV Perception.}
BEVFormer~\cite{bevformer} uses BEV queries that interact with multi-camera features via deformable spatial cross-attention and temporal self-attention (51.7\% NDS on nuScenes). BEVFormer v2~\cite{bevformerv2} adds perspective 3D supervision, letting modern ViT backbones match or exceed ResNet variants. Lift-Splat-Shoot~\cite{lss} and BEVDepth use explicit depth prediction; BEVFusion~\cite{bevfusion} combines camera and LiDAR.

\subsection{Visual Foundation Models.}
DINO~\cite{dino} and DINOv2~\cite{dinov2} use self-distillation on large image corpora to produce spatially structured features without labels. V-JEPA~\cite{vjepa} extends JEPA to video, training an encoder to predict representations of masked video tubes from context, producing features sensitive to temporal dynamics. Their utility as frozen feature providers for geometric tasks like BEV projection is underexplored.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────
\section{3. Method}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{3.1 System Overview.}
BEVFormer's ResNet-101 backbone produces a four-scale feature pyramid at strides $\{8, 16, 32, 64\}$, all of which feed the BEV projection. We replace this with a single frozen foundation model---V-JEPA~2.1 (ViT-B/16) or DINOv3 (ViT-B/14)---whose weights are never updated. A lightweight \emph{adapter} converts the frozen patch tokens into spatial feature maps; BEVFormer's existing projection lifts them into a $200\!\times\!200$ BEV grid. Only the adapter and task heads are trained.

\subsection{3.2 Adapter Design.}
V-JEPA and DINOv3 produce frozen ViT patch tokens of dimension 768 on a coarse grid. Unlike CNN/FPN features, these tokens come from global self-attention and therefore carry a weaker local spatial bias---a mismatch with BEVFormer's deformable cross-attention, which expects dense convolutional maps where neighbouring locations encode neighbouring image regions. The adapter must both reduce 768$\to$256 channels and refine the tokens into a locally consistent spatial map.

A purely linear projection is limited because it processes each token independently and cannot model interactions between neighboring patches. Adding local convolutions directly addresses this limitation by mixing information across nearby tokens on the patch grid, which leads to a clear performance improvement. We also explored a gated temporal fusion variant, where multiple cached V-JEPA temporal slices are combined through a learned per-token gate. This initially improved over naive temporal reduction, but the gain was not consistent in later experiments and the gated variant sometimes degraded performance once stronger adapters and higher-resolution features were introduced. This suggests that the dominant bottleneck is not simply how temporal slices are weighted, but whether the frozen patch tokens are sufficiently refined into a spatial feature map compatible with BEVFormer. In our final design, four residual convolutional blocks with hidden dimension 512 provided the best trade-off between spatial refinement, stability, and computational cost.


\subsection{3.3 Feature Resolution.}
 
ViT-B/16 processes images using non-overlapping patches of $16 \times 16$ pixels. Our default $448 \times 800$ input yields a grid of $28 \times 50$ tokens, scaling spatial resolution linearly with input size. This denser representation directly benefits BEV projection by reducing quantization errors during deformable attention sampling at sub-patch coordinates.
 
We evaluate four resolutions: $224\times384$ (6\% of native), $368\times656$ (17.6\%), $448\times800$ (25\%), and $896\times1600$ (full). NDS improves from 0.344 at $224\times384$ to 0.375 at $448\times800$, but plateaus at full resolution (0.375). Given our project's time and compute constraints, the marginal returns of full resolution do not justify the higher caching and training overhead. Although full resolution remains technically feasible under our hardware limits, the smaller footprint of $448\times800$ offers a highly practical compromise suitable for edge deployment.

\subsection{3.4 Deformable Attention Sampling.}
 
BEVFormer's original deformable cross-attention samples 8 reference points
\textit{per scale} across its four-level ResNet FPN, for a total of 32 sampled
locations per BEV query. Since we replace the FPN with a single-scale ViT
feature map, we sample only 8 reference points total. We swept $\{8, 12, 16\}$
and found no meaningful improvement beyond 8: at stride-16, nearby reference
points frequently fall within the same patch and retrieve identical token
features, making additional samples redundant.


\subsection{3.5 Temporal BEV Attention.}
 Detecting objects in 3D requires more than spatial features --- the model must
also estimate velocity and heading, which cannot be inferred from a single
frame. BEVFormer addresses this by maintaining a queue of past BEV grids,
each warped to the current ego position via odometry, and attending over them
to track how the scene evolves over time.
This component is also the key variable that separates V-JEPA from DINOv3 in
our study: since V-JEPA is pretrained on video it carries some temporal signal
in its tokens, while DINOv3 is purely spatial. Ablating temporal BEV attention
therefore reveals how much each backbone can recover on its own versus how
much it relies on BEV-level fusion --- results in Section~4.4.
 \subsection{3.5 Multi-Task Heads.}
We attach three auxiliary heads to the shared BEV grid: a convolutional
\textbf{map segmentation head} (6 nuScenes semantic classes), an
\textbf{LSTM tracking head} that propagates detection queries across frames,
and an MLP \textbf{ego-trajectory head} regressing 6 future waypoints.
All heads share the same BEV features with no task-specific decoders,
so their gradients compete over the same representation --- consequences
analysed in Section~4.5.

\section{4. Experiments}

Training and evaluation uses nuScenes~\cite{nuscenes} (700/150 train/val scenes, 6 surround cameras). The primary metric is NDS, a composite of mAP, mATE, mASE, mAOE, mAVE, mAAE. All models train on 4$\times$ A100 80\,GB, batch size 4, up to 30 epochs. Latency is measured on a single A100 at batch size 1.

\subsection{4.3 Latency Advantage.}
Both foundation-model configurations run at $\sim$\textbf{230\,ms / sample} vs.\ \textbf{530\,ms} for BEVFormer---a \textbf{2.3$\times$} speedup. Frozen features are pre-cached as H5 tensors so inference skips the backbone entirely, and ViT-B with our Conv+4Res adapter ($\sim$12M trainable params) is far lighter than ResNet-101 with deformable convolutions. V-JEPA reaches \textbf{83.5\%} of BEVFormer NDS at \textbf{43\%} of its latency; DINOv3 reaches \textbf{90.1\%}. For onboard or fleet deployment where perception must finish within a sensor frame window, this trade-off is highly favourable.

\subsection{4.4 The Critical Role of Temporal BEV Attention.}
The most striking result in the study is the collapse of DINOv3 \emph{without} temporal BEV attention: NDS drops to 0.286 (mAVE 1.109, mAOE 0.606), recovering to 0.466 once temporal attention is added---an 18-point swing, the largest single-factor gain we observe.

\textbf{Velocity and orientation are the failure modes.} mAVE rises from 0.461 to 1.109 ($\sim$3$\times$) and mAOE from 0.445 to 0.606 (+36\%) when temporal attention is removed. Neither can be estimated from a single frame: a parked and a moving car look identical, and motion history is what disambiguates both heading and speed. BEVFormer's temporal attention aligns BEV grids across time and effectively provides optical flow at the BEV level. V-JEPA is partially robust because its video pretraining embeds some velocity signal (mAVE without temporal BEV: 0.663 vs.\ DINOv3's 1.109), but the partial substitute does not eliminate the need for BEV-level fusion. \textbf{BEV-level temporal modelling is non-negotiable for camera-only 3D perception.}

\subsection{4.5 Multi-Task Learning Analysis.}
Training V-JEPA with all four task heads simultaneously yields only 0.290 NDS for detection at \textbf{epoch 6} (training not yet converged). Despite this, the model already shows meaningful multi-task behaviour: segmentation produces structured drivable-area and walkway masks, and tracking propagates identities across frames.

Selective combinations reveal the gradient-interference pattern. Detection + tracking + ego-trajectory retains \textbf{0.405 NDS} (a 0.026 cost over detection-only) while adding full motion estimation. Detection + map segmentation drops to \textbf{0.360 NDS}---a larger cost---because segmentation gradients push BEV features toward semantic boundary sharpness rather than geometric localisation precision. Future work should explore gradient surgery~\cite{grad_surgery} or uncertainty-weighted loss balancing.

\section{5. Discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

Our results raise two deeper questions: why does V-JEPA underperform DINOv3 despite its temporal pretraining, and why do both frozen models fall short of the BEVFormer SOTA?

\subsection{5.1 Why V-JEPA Underperforms DINOv3.}
On the surface, V-JEPA looks better suited for AV: it understands video, motion, and dynamics. Yet DINOv3 wins across all configurations (0.466 vs.\ 0.431 at best). Three factors explain this.

\textbf{BEV projection is primarily a dense geometric problem, not a temporal one.} Deformable cross-attention samples sparse reference points in image space; every sampled location must carry accurate local geometry so that the BEV query places the object at the correct 3D position. DINOv3's self-distillation directly optimises spatial discriminability across the image plane and produces features with sharp spatial structure. V-JEPA's predictive objective rewards more global, contextual representations---useful for dynamics, less precise for pixel-level localisation.

\textbf{V-JEPA features are temporally entangled.} V-JEPA processes a clip of $T\!=\!2$ frames jointly; its token representations mix spatial and temporal information. When we extract features for BEVFormer, we use only the spatial tokens from the target frame. However, these tokens have been influenced by the neighboring frames during the self-attention computation inside V-JEPA, meaning their spatial meaning is partially diluted by temporal context. This can hurt the precision of deformable attention sampling: a token that ``knows'' about the motion of a pedestrian over 2 frames may not pinpoint the pedestrian's exact spatial location within the current frame as sharply as a purely spatial encoder would.

\textbf{Domain gap.} V-JEPA 2.1 is trained predominantly on web video (Kinetics / HowTo100M-style), with substantially different statistics from automotive forward cameras. DINOv3, trained on a curated billion-image corpus, has broader photometric coverage closer to nuScenes' fisheye-corrected automotive imagery.

A more tailored integration---using V-JEPA's attention maps as motion priors for deformable offsets, or routing V-JEPA specifically to the tracking and velocity heads---could better exploit its temporal properties.

\textbf{Implications.} These findings suggest that V-JEPA's temporal knowledge is not effectively exploited by BEVFormer's existing BEV temporal attention module, which operates at the BEV feature level rather than consuming V-JEPA's cross-frame token relationships directly. A more tailored integration---for example, using V-JEPA's attention maps as motion priors for the deformable sampling offsets, or routing V-JEPA features specifically to the tracking and velocity heads---could better exploit its temporal properties.


\subsection{5.2 Why Neither Model Reaches BEVFormer SOTA.}

Our best configuration (DINOv3, 0.466 NDS) falls 5.1 NDS points short of BEVFormer v1's 0.517 baseline~\cite{bevformer}. Rather than a deficit in backbone quality, this gap is a structural byproduct of our design choices: frozen weights, resolution downsampling, and reduced sampling density.

\textbf{Feature Adaptation and the Frozen Backbone.} While BEVFormer v1 fine-tunes its ResNet-101 end-to-end with BEV 3D detection loss, our backbones remain frozen. End-to-end gradients shape backbone features specifically for perspective-to-BEV transformation (e.g., learning perspective depth cues and object scale boundaries). Our lightweight Conv+4Res adapter ($\sim$12M parameters) cannot fully re-purpose general-purpose, frozen 768-dimensional tokens into these highly specialized multi-scale spatial representations.

\textbf{Resolution and Sampling Bottlenecks.} Our models use a $4 \times$ downsampled resolution of $448 \times 800$ (yielding a token grid of $28 \times 50$ patches) and 8 deformable attention sampling points, whereas BEVFormer v1 utilizes $900 \times 1600$ images and 32 sampling points. The stride-16 patch tokenization inherently misses high-frequency, stride-8 spatial details needed for precise boundary localization. While early isolated ablations of full resolution and higher sampling density showed minimal gain, these were conducted without the temporal BEV module. Combined with temporal context and backbone unfreezing, higher resolution and denser sampling are likely necessary to close the localization gap, though at a significant latency cost.

\textbf{Missing Perspective Supervision.} Finally, as established by BEVFormer v2~\cite{bevformerv2}, optimal adaptation of ViT encoders to BEV projection requires auxiliary perspective 3D supervision during backbone training to teach the encoder projective geometry and depth cues. Lacking this auxiliary perspective signal, alongside frozen weights and compressed resolutions, constitutes a hard ceiling on our models' geometric capabilities.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────
\section{6. Conclusion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

We presented VJEPA-BEV, a systematic study of frozen foundation-model features for BEV perception on nuScenes. A staged ablation yields 0.431 NDS for V-JEPA and 0.466 NDS for DINOv3, both at 230\,ms/sample versus 530\,ms for the BEVFormer reference. DINOv3 outperforms V-JEPA because BEV projection demands spatially precise, non-entangled features---something image-level self-distillation produces more directly than V-JEPA's temporally predictive objective. Neither model reaches BEVFormer SOTA, primarily because of frozen-backbone non-adaptation, our $448\!\times\!800$ / stride-16 / 8-point compression, and the absence of perspective 3D supervision. Frozen foundation-model backbones nonetheless offer a compelling 2.3$\times$ efficiency advantage at 83--90\% of SOTA NDS, making them attractive for latency-constrained deployment.
