\section{Abstract}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────
We investigate whether frozen visual foundation models can serve as robust backbones for multi-task Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) perception, bypassing the need for computationally expensive, end-to-end task-specific training. Pitting a video world model (V-JEPA 2.1) against a purely spatial encoder (DINOv3) inside the BEVFormer framework, we systematically map their representational trade-offs. Our results demonstrate that both frozen encoders achieve highly competitive 3D detection performance at a fraction of the baseline's latency. However, our analysis reveals that high-fidelity spatial pretraining is far more critical than video-level temporal pretraining for resolving the geometric view-projection bottleneck. Furthermore, we show that temporal context is most effectively integrated at the BEV query level, and when scaling to joint multi-task learning, the backbone's pretraining bias strongly dictates representation stability under gradient conflict.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────
\section{1. Introduction}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) perception has become a central paradigm for camera-only autonomous driving because it maps multi-camera observations into a metric top-down space directly consumable by downstream planning modules. BEVFormer~\cite{bevformer} established a strong baseline using deformable cross-attention to lift ResNet perspective features into a BEV grid, augmented with recurrent temporal self-attention. Its ResNet-101 backbone, however, is relatively shallow and trained only with detection labels---it lacks the broad semantic and geometric representations modern self-supervised foundation models provide.

\subsection{1.1 Why BEV?}

We motivate BEV as our substrate on three grounds beyond benchmark performance.

\textbf{Unification across cameras and modalities.} A safe AV requires 360$^\circ$ awareness at every time step. BEV projects all surround cameras into a shared metric frame, enabling joint reasoning across views that single-camera approaches~\cite{drivejepa} structurally cannot. The same frame admits LiDAR fusion (BEVFusion~\cite{bevfusion}), so the camera-only architecture studied here extends to multi-modal settings without redesign.

\textbf{Composable heads and interpretability.} Each downstream task reduces to a query plus a small decoder over the shared BEV tensor: we drive four heads from a single $200\!\times\!200$ map, and occupancy or planning would extend the same way. The geometric intermediate is also inspectable---after a failure the BEV map attributes the cause to an upstream miss (element absent from BEV) or a downstream miss (element present but mis-decoded), an introspection surface that end-to-end pixel-to-control architectures lack.

Our primary target is V-JEPA~2.1~\cite{vjepa}, a recent video foundation model trained by predicting masked patch representations across frames, and therefore equipped with temporal and physics-like dynamics knowledge---features that should be advantageous in a domain as dynamic as autonomous driving. To isolate the contribution of this video-level signal, we additionally evaluate DINOv3~\cite{dinov3} as a control: a frozen image backbone trained via self-distillation that provides rich spatial features but no temporal pretraining. If V-JEPA's temporal knowledge translates into better BEV representations, V-JEPA should outperform DINOv3; if spatial quality is the dominant bottleneck, the inverse will hold.

This paper contributes: \textbf{(i)} a staged ablation showing how adapter design, input resolution, deformable sampling, and temporal BEV fusion each affect detection quality when the backbone is frozen; \textbf{(ii)} a direct comparison of V-JEPA and DINOv3 under identical conditions, showing that spatial pretraining quality matters more than video-level temporal pretraining for BEV projection; and \textbf{(iii)} a multi-task analysis revealing that gradient interference under joint training depends heavily on backbone choice---DINOv3 handles all four heads gracefully while V-JEPA collapses under segmentation pressure.

\section{2. Related Work}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{BEV Perception.}
BEVFormer~\cite{bevformer} uses BEV queries that interact with multi-camera features via deformable spatial cross-attention and temporal self-attention (51.7\% NDS on nuScenes). BEVFormer v2~\cite{bevformerv2} adds perspective 3D supervision, letting modern ViT backbones match or exceed ResNet variants. Lift-Splat-Shoot~\cite{lss} and BEVDepth use explicit depth prediction; BEVFusion~\cite{bevfusion} combines camera and LiDAR.

\subsection{Visual Foundation Models.}
V-JEPA~\cite{vjepa} extends the Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to video, training an encoder to predict representations of masked video tubes from context to produce features sensitive to temporal dynamics. In parallel, the DINO family, including the latest DINOv3, uses self-distillation on large image corpora to produce spatially structured representations without human labels. While both encoder families transfer well in fine-tuned settings, their utility as frozen feature providers for dense geometric tasks like BEV projection remains under-explored.

\section{3. Method}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{3.1 System Overview.}
BEVFormer's ResNet-101 backbone produces a four-scale feature pyramid at strides $\{8, 16, 32, 64\}$, all of which feed the BEV projection. We replace this with a single frozen foundation model---V-JEPA~2.1 (ViT-B/16) or DINOv3 (ViT-B/14)---whose weights are never updated. A lightweight \emph{adapter} converts the frozen patch tokens into spatial feature maps; BEVFormer's existing projection lifts them into a $200\!\times\!200$ BEV grid. Only the adapter and task heads are trained.

\subsection{3.2 Adapter Design.}
V-JEPA and DINOv3 produce frozen ViT patch tokens of dimension 768 on a coarse grid. Unlike CNN/FPN features, these tokens come from global self-attention and therefore carry a weaker local spatial bias---a mismatch with BEVFormer's deformable cross-attention, which expects dense convolutional maps where neighbouring locations encode neighbouring image regions. The adapter must both reduce 768$\to$256 channels and refine the tokens into a locally consistent spatial map.

A purely linear projection is limited because it processes each token independently and cannot model interactions between neighboring patches. Adding local convolutions directly addresses this limitation by mixing information across nearby tokens on the patch grid, which leads to a clear performance improvement. We also explored a gated temporal fusion variant, where multiple cached V-JEPA temporal slices are combined through a learned per-token gate. This initially improved over naive temporal reduction, but the gain was not consistent in later experiments and the gated variant sometimes degraded performance once stronger adapters and higher-resolution features were introduced. This suggests that the dominant bottleneck is not simply how temporal slices are weighted, but whether the frozen patch tokens are sufficiently refined into a spatial feature map compatible with BEVFormer. In our final design, four residual convolutional blocks with hidden dimension 512 provided the best trade-off between spatial refinement, stability, and computational cost. Table \ref{tab:ablation} shows the complete set of ablations.


\subsection{3.3 Feature Resolution.}
 
ViT-B/16 processes images using non-overlapping patches of $16 \times 16$ pixels. Our default $448 \times 800$ input yields a grid of $28 \times 50$ tokens, scaling spatial resolution linearly with input size. This denser representation directly benefits BEV projection by reducing quantization errors during deformable attention sampling at sub-patch coordinates.
 
We evaluate four resolutions: $224\times384$ (6\% of native), $368\times656$ (17.6\%), $448\times800$ (25\%), and $896\times1600$ (full). NDS improves from 0.344 at $224\times384$ to 0.375 at $448\times800$, but plateaus at full resolution (0.375). Given our project's time and compute constraints, the marginal returns of full resolution do not justify the higher caching and training overhead. Although full resolution remains technically feasible under our hardware limits, the smaller footprint of $448\times800$ offers a highly practical compromise suitable for edge deployment.

\subsection{3.4 Deformable Attention Sampling.}
 
BEVFormer's original deformable cross-attention samples 8 reference points
\textit{per scale} across its four-level ResNet FPN, for a total of 32 sampled
locations per BEV query. Since we replace the FPN with a single-scale ViT
feature map, we sample only 8 reference points total. We swept $\{8, 12, 16\}$
and found no meaningful improvement beyond 8: at stride-16, nearby reference
points frequently fall within the same patch and retrieve identical token
features, making additional samples redundant.


\subsection{3.5 Temporal BEV Attention.}
 Detecting objects in 3D requires more than spatial features --- the model must
also estimate velocity and heading, which cannot be inferred from a single
frame. BEVFormer addresses this by maintaining a queue of past BEV grids,
each warped to the current ego position via odometry, and attending over them
to track how the scene evolves over time.
This component is also the key variable that separates V-JEPA from DINOv3 in
our study: since V-JEPA is pretrained on video it carries some temporal signal
in its tokens, while DINOv3 is purely spatial. Ablating temporal BEV attention
therefore reveals how much each backbone can recover on its own versus how
much it relies on BEV-level fusion --- results in Section~4.4.
 \subsection{3.5 Multi-Task Heads.}
We attach three auxiliary heads to the shared BEV grid: a convolutional
\textbf{map segmentation head} (6 nuScenes semantic classes), an
\textbf{LSTM tracking head} that propagates detection queries across frames,
and an MLP \textbf{ego-trajectory head} regressing 6 future waypoints.
All heads share the same BEV features with no task-specific decoders,
so their gradients compete over the same representation --- consequences
analysed in Section~4.5.

\section{4. Experiments}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{4.1 Setup.}
Training and evaluation uses nuScenes~\cite{nuscenes} (700/150 train/val scenes, 6 surround cameras). The primary metric is NDS, a composite of mAP, mATE, mASE, mAOE, mAVE, mAAE. All models train on 4$\times$ A100 80\,GB, batch size 4, up to 30 epochs. Latency is measured at batch size 1 on a single GPU; our configurations run at approximately \textbf{232\,ms per sample} (which includes the backbone forward pass) compared to \textbf{526\,ms} for the BEVFormer reference.

\subsection{4.3 Impact of Temporal BEV Attention.}
Our experiments validate the original BEVFormer findings regarding the necessity of temporal fusion for 3D perception. For the static DINOv3 encoder, removing temporal BEV attention drops NDS to 0.286, with the primary failure modes being velocity (mAVE rises to 1.109) and orientation (mAOE rises to 0.606). This behavior is expected; single-frame inputs lack the temporal cues required to estimate the heading and speed of identical-looking objects (e.g., distinguishing parked and moving vehicles). 

V-JEPA exhibits partial robustness to this temporal removal due to its video pretraining, maintaining an mAVE of 0.663 (compared to DINOv3's 1.109) even without BEV-level temporal attention. However, this inherent motion signal is not a complete substitute for multi-frame BEV-level alignment. These results reinforce the consensus that explicit BEV-level temporal modeling is critical for camera-only 3D state estimation.

\subsection{4.4 Multi-Task Learning Analysis.}
We extend our evaluation to joint multi-task learning by introducing auxiliary heads for map segmentation, tracking, and ego-trajectory forecasting. Under this unified regime, we observe distinct trade-offs in how each encoder handles the competing objectives (Tab.~\ref{tab:main}).

For the DINOv3 backbone, the shared BEV representation generalizes relatively well across all four heads, maintaining a strong detection score of \textbf{0.459 NDS} (mAP: 0.348). In contrast, the V-JEPA configuration experiences a severe performance degradation when joint map segmentation is added, with its detection score collapsing to \textbf{0.279 NDS} (mAP: 0.153). This confirms that forcing all tasks to share a single, frozen BEV representation can create substantial gradient interference, particularly when the backbone weights cannot be fine-tuned to resolve task conflict.

Interestingly, our results reveal that task compatibility is closely linked to the encoder's pretraining bias. When map segmentation is excluded, the V-JEPA configuration (detection + tracking + ego-trajectory) retains a much healthier detection score of \textbf{0.405 NDS}. Furthermore, on the highly dynamic ego-trajectory forecasting task, V-JEPA consistently outperforms DINOv3. In the 4-task setting, V-JEPA achieves a lower average trajectory error (\textbf{1.324\,m} Ego ADE and \textbf{2.560\,m} Ego FDE) than DINOv3 (\textbf{1.533\,m} and \textbf{2.971\,m}), despite having a lower object object detection score. This indicates that V-JEPA's motion-centric pretraining prioritizes dynamic, trajectory-relevant properties over static, pixel-level classification, which makes static map segmentation a particularly harsh distractor for its representations.

\section{5. Discussion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

Our results raise two deeper questions: why does V-JEPA underperform DINOv3 despite its temporal pretraining, and why do both frozen models fall short of the BEVFormer SOTA?

\subsection{5.1 Why V-JEPA Underperforms DINOv3.}
On the surface, V-JEPA looks better suited for AV: it understands video, motion, and dynamics. Yet DINOv3 wins across all configurations (0.466 vs.\ 0.431 at best). Three factors explain this.

\textbf{BEV projection is primarily a dense geometric problem, not a temporal one.} Deformable cross-attention samples sparse reference points in image space; every sampled location must carry accurate local geometry so that the BEV query places the object at the correct 3D position. DINOv3's self-distillation directly optimises spatial discriminability across the image plane and produces features with sharp spatial structure. V-JEPA's predictive objective rewards more global, contextual representations---useful for dynamics, less precise for pixel-level localisation.

\textbf{V-JEPA features carry motion context that blurs spatial precision.} V-JEPA processes multiple frames at once, so each token reflects not just the current patch but also how the scene is moving around it. When we extract features for a single frame, this cross-frame motion signal is baked in. For BEVFormer, which needs to know where something is right now with pixel-level precision, this is a liability: a token describing a pedestrian encodes where it was and where it is going, not just where it is, making the spatial signal less sharp than that of a purely image-level encoder like DINOv3.

\textbf{Domain gap.} V-JEPA 2.1 is trained predominantly on web video (Kinetics / HowTo100M-style), with substantially different statistics from automotive forward cameras. DINOv3, trained on a curated billion-image corpus, has broader photometric coverage closer to nuScenes' fisheye-corrected automotive imagery.

A more tailored integration, using V-JEPA's attention maps as motion priors for deformable offsets, or routing V-JEPA specifically to the tracking and velocity heads, could better exploit its temporal properties.

\subsection{5.2 Why Neither Model Reaches BEVFormer SOTA.}

Our best configuration (DINOv3, 0.466 NDS) falls 5.1 NDS points short of BEVFormer v1's 0.517 baseline~\cite{bevformer}. Rather than a deficit in backbone quality, this gap is a structural byproduct of our design choices: frozen weights, resolution downsampling, and reduced sampling density.

\textbf{Feature Adaptation and the Frozen Backbone.} While BEVFormer v1 fine-tunes its ResNet-101 end-to-end with BEV 3D detection loss, our backbones remain frozen. End-to-end gradients shape backbone features specifically for perspective-to-BEV transformation (e.g., learning perspective depth cues and object scale boundaries). Our lightweight Conv+4Res adapter cannot fully translate general-purpose, frozen 768-dimensional tokens into these highly specialized multi-scale spatial representations.

\textbf{Resolution and Sampling Bottlenecks.} As discussed in Sections 3.3 and 3.4, our configurations limit spatial input to $448 \times 800$ (yielding $28 \times 50$ tokens) and utilize only 8 deformable sampling points, whereas BEVFormer v1 uses native $900 \times 1600$ images and 32 sampling points. While our offline ablations showed diminishing returns when scaling up resolution and sampling density, we expect that an unfrozen backbone paired with full resolution could learn to leverage these fine-grained spatial details directly, ultimately helping to narrow the translation error (mATE) gap.

\textbf{Missing Perspective Supervision.} Finally, as established by BEVFormer v2~\cite{bevformerv2}, adapting modern ViTs to BEV tasks optimally requires explicit perspective 3D supervision during backbone training to teach the encoder projective geometry and depth cues. Lacking this auxiliary perspective signal, alongside frozen weights and compressed resolutions, constitutes a hard ceiling on our models' geometric capabilities.

\section{6. Conclusion}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────

% We presented VJEPA-BEV, a systematic study of frozen foundation-model features for BEV perception on nuScenes. A staged ablation yields 0.431 NDS for V-JEPA and 0.466 NDS for DINOv3, both at 230\,ms/sample versus 530\,ms for the BEVFormer reference. DINOv3 outperforms V-JEPA because BEV projection demands spatially precise, non-entangled features---something image-level self-distillation produces more directly than V-JEPA's temporally predictive objective. Neither model reaches BEVFormer SOTA, primarily because of frozen-backbone non-adaptation, our $448\!\times\!800$ / stride-16 / 8-point compression, and the absence of perspective 3D supervision. Frozen foundation-model backbones nonetheless offer a compelling 2.3$\times$ efficiency advantage at 83--90\% of SOTA NDS, making them attractive for latency-constrained deployment.

We presented VJEPA-BEV, a systematic study of frozen foundation-model features for multi-task BEV perception on nuScenes. Our exploration reveals that while both frozen DINOv3 and V-JEPA 2.1 provide highly competitive baselines at lower latency than a fully fine-tuned BEVFormer, their representational trade-offs vary significantly. For the core BEV projection, we find that static spatial geometry is the primary bottleneck; here, DINOv3's sharp spatial features outperform V-JEPA's temporally entangled representations. However, when scaling to a generalist multi-task setting, V-JEPA's video-level temporal pretraining provides a distinct advantage for dynamic heads (ego-trajectory forecasting and tracking), whereas static tasks like map segmentation act as harsh gradient distractors. Ultimately, our results suggest that while frozen backbones are highly viable, a single shared BEV grid has structural limits. Designing a true generalist AV model requires carefully balancing spatial and temporal pretraining biases alongside architectural routing to mitigate multi-task gradient conflict.
